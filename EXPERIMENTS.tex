\noindent\textbf{Results in hand}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Core attack validation across generators (4 attacks $\times$ 4 generators)} \emph{[partial]} MahalaMIA, MAMA-MIA, MeLoMIA-ND and MeLoMIA-CVAE scored on MVN, CVAE, NoisyDiffusion, and DP-PGM, both cohorts, 5 splits; validates reproducibility of abstract and identifies attack--mechanism mismatches (MahalaMIA targets geometry, MAMA-MIA targets marginals). Against the corrected DP-PGM, MAMA-MIA rises above chance while MahalaMIA stays at chance on both cohorts (0.504, 0.505), which is the cleanest evidence for the mismatch. \emph{Still open:} both MeLoMIA rows against the corrected DP-PGM (the existing cells measured the broken generator), and matched base shadows for the off-diagonal MeLoMIA cells, which are lower bounds until then (see below).
\item \textbf{Shadow budget and meta-classifier design (K=5 vs.\ 30)} \emph{[partial]} Increasing shadow count from 5 to 30 reverses attack ordering: ND gains +0.238 AUC, CVAE only +0.045, showing shadow count is a per-attack tuning axis. \emph{Still open:} the full curve over $K \in \{5,10,30,50\}$ (config \texttt{ablation\_shadow\_count} is written, never run).
\item \textbf{Shadow training data: real splits vs synthetic} \emph{[partial: BRCA]} Synth-shadows outperform real-data shadows by +0.161 AUC on MeLoMIA-ND; but grouped CV ranks them backwards due to self-memorization, showing CV cannot reliably estimate deployment performance. \emph{Still open:} COMBINED.
\item \textbf{Model-disjoint validation (the internal-proxy role)} \emph{[done; negative] (new)} Holding whole shadow models out lowers CV AUC (ND $-0.023$, CVAE $-0.010$) and shrinks the chosen feature budgets, but no grid cell moves by more than 0.015. Selection was on the wrong axis and it did not matter. Pairs with the previous item: never quote a MeLoMIA CV AUC as an attack result.
\item \textbf{Utility validation (TSTR, Wasserstein, discriminator)} \emph{[done]} Baseline quality checks on all generators; catches structural issues (e.g., DP-PGM gene-order scramble dropped TSTR-F1 to 0.092).
\item \textbf{MahalaMIA covariance conditioning} \emph{[done]} Sweep ridge alpha, Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage, pseudo-inverse, and PCA truncation; the n/p crossover flips the best conditioner (ridge below 1.0, shrinkage above). Dropping leading components separates CVAE (low-variance leak) from ND (high-variance leak). Class-conditional scoring closes MVN to AUC 1.000 on COMBINED.
\item \textbf{Cohort size as n/p} \emph{[done: MahalaMIA $\times$ MVN, CVAE] (split out of the datasets item)} COMBINED resampled to $n = 500 \ldots 3458$ at $p = 978$: exposure is a smooth function of $n/p$, perfect at $n \le p$. The submitted pseudo-inverse is non-monotone and understates risk exactly where risk is highest.
\item \textbf{MAMA-MIA aggregation and scoring (DP-PGM)} \emph{[done; defaults not yet changed]} Log-ratios beat ratios (+2.1 AUC, +6.6 pts TPR@10\%FPR on BRCA). Subtype confounds the gene$\times$label family, because a candidate's score there depends on their label and label predicts class, not membership. On BRCA, class-centring per subtype fixes it (+4.5 AUC). On COMBINED (12 classes) centring does not rescue the family and 1-way marginals alone win (+4.1 AUC). Inverse-variance weighting hurts. \emph{Caveat:} class-centring is transductive, since it uses the other candidates' scores.
\item \textbf{Generator-specific leaks} \emph{[done; ND write-up open]} NoisyDiffusion inverts a \texttt{QuantileTransformer} fitted on the training split, reaching AUC 0.9996 (post-processing leak). DP-PGM had three accounting defects (permuted genes, basic vs zCDP, sensitivity mismatch) that made it appear private. The corrected version reaches AUC 0.604 BRCA / 0.530 COMBINED with the shipped attack, and 0.671 / 0.586 with the better aggregation, against analytical ceilings of 0.892 / 0.874.
\end{itemize}

\noindent\textbf{Open --- DP-PGM}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Epsilon--AUC curve (DP-PGM, $\epsilon = 0.1$ to 50)} \emph{[open]} Empirically validate noise scaling against corrected accounting; show privacy--utility frontier and reveal whether attack AUC ceiling holds at all budgets. Plot the analytical SNR ceiling on the same axes, since it is free and tells us how much of the gap is the attack's to close.
\item \textbf{Discretization accounting} \emph{[done]} The legacy edges are percentiles of the training data and spend nothing, and the attack beats the $\epsilon$ bound because of it ($0.620$ vs $0.523$ at $\epsilon=0.3$ on BRCA, and the curve is flat over $\epsilon \in [0.3, 3]$). Three DP-valid binnings are implemented and swept: \texttt{uniform} (equal width over a public range), \texttt{dp\_uniform} (DP bounds then equal width, which is what smartnoise-synth's \texttt{BinTransformer} does for MST and AIM) and \texttt{dp\_quantile} (DP equal depth at 10\% of $\rho$). Under all three, nothing exceeds the bound. FINDINGS \S10.
\item \textbf{Bin-edge recovery} \emph{[open]} Decoding dithers uniformly within each bin, so the released data has density jumps at the edges. Under \texttt{dp\_quantile} the edges are data-dependent, so recovering them from the release is a genuine black-box attack; the white-box row (the attack given the generator's cells) bounds what it can win. Full end-to-end $\epsilon$ still excludes marginal selection below $n_{1\text{way}} = 978$.
\item \textbf{DP-PGM with data-dependent marginal selection} \emph{[open] (new)} At $n_{1\text{way}} < 978$ or $n_{2\text{way}} > 0$ the selected marginals depend on the private data. That is the only DP-PGM setting where MAMA-MIA's shadow machinery has a job (discovering the focal points), and where selection is a second unbudgeted leak.
\item \textbf{DP-PGM at small n} \emph{[open] (new)} Does the guarantee still bite when $n < p$, where MVN and CVAE become perfectly attackable?
\end{itemize}

\noindent\textbf{Open --- MeLoMIA and threat model}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Threat model variation: black-box vs white-box} \emph{[open]} Black-box (current): adversary sees only released synthetic data; builds proxy on its own synthetic shadows. White-box: direct access to target model, collapsing all shadow layers. \emph{Correction:} \texttt{synth\_shadow: false} covers only the shadow side (shadows trained on real splits). The scoring side, extracting features from the target itself rather than from a proxy fitted to $D_\text{synth}$, is not implemented, and the ND column needs \texttt{--retrain-nd} targets so that we hold the weights.
\item \textbf{Matched base shadows for off-diagonal cells} \emph{[open] (new)} Off the diagonal, MeLoMIA's base shadows come from the probe's family, not the target's. That is the domain gap synth-shadow modelling exists to close. \texttt{MeLoMIA(base\_generator=...)} is implemented but has not been run.
\item \textbf{Which part of the loss trajectory carries membership} \emph{[open] (new)} ND timesteps (low / mid / high $t$), CVAE temperatures, draw budget $N$. Config \texttt{ablation\_sweep\_axis} is written and mostly re-slices cached features.
\item \textbf{ND's SMOTE layer as a confound} \emph{[open] (new)} ND upsamples every class to 3000 rows (17$\times$ on BRCA, 10$\times$ on COMBINED). This blocks the ND cohort-size arm and may explain part of ND's resistance. Re-run one ND column with SMOTE off.
\end{itemize}

\noindent\textbf{Open --- scope and new models}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Additional datasets and cohort sizes} \emph{[open]} The ND arm (after SMOTE), the MeLoMIA attacks at varying $n$, genuinely different cohorts. Cross-dataset transfer: train on one cohort, deploy on independent external RNA-seq data to measure attack generalization.
\item \textbf{Vine copula and rigorous MahalaMIA study} \emph{[open]} Replace simple Gaussian covariance with vine copulas to capture tail dependence and asymmetry in gene expression; benchmark against current ridge + class-conditional MahalaMIA. Sweep copula families and parameterizations and measure sensitivity to model misspecification. \emph{Scope note:} MahalaMIA is already strongest on MVN and CVAE (1.000, 0.996), but not on ND (0.826, vs.\ MeLoMIA-ND 0.858 and MAMA-MIA 0.9996), so ND is where a copula has something to prove. At 978 dimensions this likely needs a truncated vine.
\item \textbf{MAMA-MIA as a nearest-value attack on ND} \emph{[open] (new)} AUC climbs with bin count (0.54 at 4 bins to 0.76 at 64). Find where it tops out, and decide whether to present it as MAMA-MIA or as a separate quantised baseline. Belongs with the ND quantile-leak write-up.
\item \textbf{Resolve the abstract's MahalaMIA vs ND number} \emph{[open] (new)} The abstract has 0.489; we get 0.806 on the blue team's own synthetic data and splits. Must be settled before the paper repeats either number.
\end{itemize}
